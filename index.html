1. Executive Summary: Solving the AISI Enforcement Gap

As documented by the Ada Lovelace Institute, national AI Safety Institutes (AISIs) in middle-power jurisdictions face a structural vulnerability: because pre-deployment model evaluations rely on voluntary memoranda of understanding, frontier labs racing toward transformative AI can withhold access, compress testing timelines, or deploy across borders without enforceable red lines. Meanwhile, consumer-market regulations struggle to police general-purpose AI (GPAI) once weights and APIs proliferate globally.

Malaysia represents a different, far more potent middle-power archetype: the Physical Infrastructure & Supply-Chain Swing State. Situated at the maritime and digital choke point of the Indo-Pacific, Malaysia controls two physical bottlenecks indispensable to both the United States and China:

  • Upstream Semiconductor Packaging (Penang & Kulim): Malaysia commands 13% of global Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT) capacity and over 20% of U.S. semiconductor imports, hosting critical advanced 2.5D/3D chiplet packaging complexes (Intel’s $7B Pelican EMIB/Foveros facility, ASE, Amkor, Inari Amertron, Infineon) backed by the RM25 billion ($5.8B) National Semiconductor Strategy (NSS).
  • Downstream Gigawatt AI Compute (Johor): Within the Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ), Johor (Sedenak Tech Park, Kulai, Nusajaya) has accumulated >3.24 GW of committed AI data center capacity ($18B+ capital inflows), uniquely co-hosting both U.S. hyperscalers (Microsoft, Nvidia/YTL Power, AWS, Oracle) and Chinese hyperscale tenants (ByteDance, GDS/Alibaba affiliates) just 2 km across the causeway from Singapore’s regional cloud core and the Singapore AI Safety Institute (aisafety.sg).

Project SELAT ("Strait Enforcement & Leverage for AI Trust") converts Malaysia’s physical electricity and customs valves into a mandatory verification credential. By linking Malaysia’s Strategic Trade Act 2010 (Act 708, Section 12) WMD non-proliferation permits with Tenaga Nasional Berhad’s (TNB) Green Lane Pathway grid interconnects, Malaysia requires any hyperscale cluster in Johor to operate under an IAEA-inspired Zero-Knowledge Safeguards Regime and pass joint NAIO + Singapore/UK AISI red-line evaluations prior to regional API deployment.
