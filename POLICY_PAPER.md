## 1. Executive Summary & Core Thesis: Closing the AISI Enforcement Gap

As highlighted by the **Ada Lovelace Institute’s** analysis of national AI Safety Institutes (`adalovelaceinstitute.org/feature/aisi/`), middle-power AI governance faces a critical structural vulnerability: **the Voluntary Enforcement Gap**. Because national AISIs rely on voluntary memoranda of understanding to conduct pre-deployment evaluations, frontier labs racing toward transformative AI can withhold model access, compress evaluation timelines, or route around consumer-market guardrails without facing hard physical consequences.

Simultaneously, the **Singapore AI Safety Institute (`aisafety.sg`)** has pioneered world-class evaluation science for the region—spearheading the **Singapore Consensus on Global AI Safety Research Priorities**, **Project Moonshot**, and **Multilingual/Multicultural Southeast Asian Red-Teaming**—yet Singapore physically ran out of land and electricity (<1.4 GW total data center capacity), forcing it to pause new hyperscale builds.

**Malaysia is the missing physical half of this equation.** Situated 2 kilometers across the Johor Strait from Singapore, Malaysia represents a fundamentally more potent middle-power archetype (**`middlepowers.ai`**): **the Physical Infrastructure & Supply-Chain Swing State.** 

Positioned at the geographic and digital maritime choke point of the Indo-Pacific, Malaysia controls two asymmetric physical bottlenecks indispensable to both the United States and China:
1. **The Upstream Packaging Valve (Penang & Kulim):** Malaysia commands **13% of global Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT)** capacity and accounts for over **20% of U.S. semiconductor imports**, hosting critical advanced chiplet packaging facilities (e.g., Intel’s *Pelican* EMIB/Foveros complex, ASE, Inari Amertron, Infineon) backed by the **RM25 billion (\$5.8B) National Semiconductor Strategy (NSS)**.
2. **The Downstream Gigawatt Compute Valve (Johor):** Anchored by the **Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)**, Johor (Sedenak Tech Park, Kulai, Nusajaya) has become Asia’s fastest-growing hyperscale AI compute hub, with **>3.24 GW of committed AI data center capacity** (\$18B+ in capital inflows across *YTL-Nvidia 500MW*, *Bridge DC Sedenak*, *Microsoft*, *AirTrunk 270MW*, *AWS*, and *Oracle*) uniquely co-hosting **both U.S. hyperscalers** and **Chinese hyperscale tenants** (ByteDance, GDS/Alibaba affiliates).

### The Strategic Dilemma
Malaysia faces an acute sovereignty trap. Washington’s Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) views Southeast Asian data centers as potential "offshore training sanctuaries" or transshipment hubs where restricted U.S.-origin accelerators (Nvidia B200/GB200) could be aggregated to train unverified frontier models, threatening Malaysia with country-level compute caps or secondary sanctions. Conversely, Beijing views unilateral exclusion of Chinese cloud tenants from Malaysian infrastructure as alignment with U.S. containment, threatening severe trade retaliation. Meanwhile, once high-density AI chips cross Malaysian customs and connect to **Tenaga Nasional Berhad’s (TNB)** power grid in Johor, Kuala Lumpur currently possesses **zero technical visibility** into whether a 300 MW facility is running benign commercial inference or executing a covert $>10^{26}$ FLOP frontier training run capable of autonomous replication, cyber-offense against critical infrastructure, or biological weapons design.

### The Mechanism: Project SELAT ("Strait Enforcement & Leverage for AI Trust")
Instead of choosing sides in a binary Cold War or relying on unenforceable paper declarations, Malaysia can convert its physical bottlenecks into a **sovereign verification credential**. 

**Project SELAT** integrates three existing Malaysian statutory and infrastructural instruments into a single **IAEA-inspired Compute Safeguards Regime**:
1. **Statutory Hook — Amending Directive No. 1/2025 under Section 12 of the Strategic Trade Act 2010 (Act 708):** On July 14, 2025, Malaysia’s Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) issued Directive No. 1/2025 under Section 12 ("Catch-All Control") of Act 708—Malaysia’s UNSCR 1540 WMD non-proliferation statute—requiring permits for AI chip transit suspected of WMD/military end-use. We propose **Directive No. 2/2026**, which legally defines *Unverified Frontier Training Runs ($>10^{26}$ FLOP)* and *Un-Evaluated High-Autonomy GPAI Deployment* as **Restricted Dual-Use Proliferation Activities**, transforming a one-time border customs check into a **Continuous Operating Safeguards License**.
2. **Infrastructure Leverage — Conditioning TNB’s "Green Lane Pathway" (GLP):** Data center operators in Johor desperately depend on TNB’s Green Lane Pathway, which slashes high-voltage grid connection timelines from **36–48 months down to 12 months**. Under Project SELAT, GLP fast-track power allocation and JS-SEZ tax incentives are contractually conditioned on operating as a **Safeguarded Compute Zone (SCZ)** with three-tier technical verification:
   * **Tier 1 (Out-of-Band Substation Power Telemetry):** Continuous TNB harmonic & load-signature monitoring to distinguish synchronized multi-week frontier pre-training runs from commercial inference without inspecting tenant data.
   * **Tier 2 (OSAT & Rack-Fabric Topology Attestation):** Cryptographic cluster-size attestation confirming how many accelerators are bridged via high-bandwidth NVLink/optical fabrics inside a single logical training domain.
   * **Tier 3 (Pre-Deployment AISI Red-Line Evals & Access-Vector Circuit Breakers):** Mandatory third-party evaluations conducted by the **National AI Office (NAIO / AI Malaysia Berhad)**—under mutual recognition with the UK and Singapore AI Safety Institutes—testing for **agentic scheming/deceptive alignment, autonomous cyber exploitation, and CBRN capability thresholds** before model weights or API endpoints can traverse Johor–Singapore dark-fiber cross-connects.
3. **Diplomatic Statecraft — The "Johor Safeguards Bargain":** Malaysia offers Washington **verifiable hardware non-proliferation** (eliminating illicit transshipment and covert $>10^{26}$ FLOP training in exchange for permanent U.S. BIS Tier-1 / Validated End-User status) while offering Beijing **non-discriminatory commercial cloud access** (Chinese firms retain legal access to Johor compute for verified commercial workloads under objective Malaysian rules rather than arbitrary U.S. extraterritorial bans).

## 4. Technical Architecture: Adapting IAEA Nuclear Safeguards to Johor Compute Clusters

A central lesson from nuclear arms control is that **sovereign declarations fail without physical, tamper-evident verification focused on choke-point materials and facilities**. Yet AI verification differs from nuclear verification in a critical respect: hyperscalers and foreign governments will fiercely resist inspections that expose proprietary model weights, training data, or commercial customer queries.

Specifically, Malaysia’s situation parallels **IAEA INFCIRC/66 (Item-Specific Safeguards)** and the **Additional Protocol (INFCIRC/540)**: Malaysia is a non-frontier-lab host state ("non-nuclear-weapon state") receiving dual-use technology (advanced GPUs and gigawatt power infrastructure) from supplier states and operating enrichment-scale facilities (hyperscale data centers) on its soil.

Project SELAT resolves the **Verification–Confidentiality Dilemma** through a **Three-Layer Zero-Knowledge Safeguards Architecture**:

### Layer 1: Out-of-Band Substation Power & Thermal Telemetry (TNB Physical Layer)
Just as IAEA inspectors monitor centrifuge cascade power consumption and UF6 flow rates at nuclear facilities, **Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB)** controls the 132kV and 275kV high-voltage substations feeding every hyperscale campus in Johor (Sedenak, Kulai, Nusajaya).
* **The Physics of Frontier Training vs. Inference:** A commercial cloud inference cluster exhibits diurnal, spiky power load curves correlated with regional user traffic (typically 55–75% average utilization with high variance). By contrast, a monolithic frontier pre-training run ($>10^{25.5}$ to $10^{26}$ FLOP across 16,000–100,000 GPUs) produces an unmistakable **step-function load signature**: weeks of sustained 92–98% peak real power draw punctuated by sharp, periodic, synchronized 40–60 second power drops every few hours during distributed AllReduce gradient synchronization and checkpoint writes to storage.
* **Zero-Knowledge Privacy:** TNB’s smart substation telemetry analyzes 1-second active/reactive power harmonics and cooling-loop thermal rejection *entirely outside the data center perimeter*. It reveals **zero customer data or model weights**, while detecting undeclared synchronized training runs with $>97\%$ statistical confidence.

### Layer 2: Inter-Rack Fabric & Hardware Inventory Attestation (MITI / CyberSecurity Malaysia Layer)
To prevent an operator from masking a frontier training run by splitting it across multiple halls or smuggling export-controlled accelerators out of the facility:
* **Cryptographic Supply-Chain Binding:** Building on Directive No. 1/2025, every shipment of advanced AI accelerators entering Johor under a Strategic Trade Permit has its hardware root-of-trust (e.g., Nvidia Hopper/Blackwell Confidential Computing attestation keys) logged in MITI’s **Strategic Compute Registry**.
* **Automated Fabric-Size Ping:** Rather than inspecting code, an on-premise tamper-evident **NAIO Verification Appliance** queries the cluster’s fabric manager (InfiniBand / RoCE / NVLink switch tables) via read-only cryptographic attestation to verify the maximum connected **bisection bandwidth domain**. If an operator bridges $>4,096$ accelerators into a single high-bandwidth training fabric without a declared **Frontier Training Safeguards Permit**, an automatic compliance alert triggers.

### Layer 3: Pre-Deployment AISI Evaluation Escrow & Scheming Stress-Tests (NAIO Layer)
When a declared frontier training or large-scale fine-tuning run crosses the statutory compute threshold ($10^{25.5}$ FLOP) or capability heuristic inside a Johor Safeguarded Compute Zone:
1. **Cryptographic Weight Escrow:** Upon completion of training checkpoints, weight hashes are committed to an isolated local escrow enclave prior to external API exposure or cross-border weight export.
2. **Automated Red-Line Evaluation Suite (Equistamp / AISI Protocol):** Through a joint technical protocol between **NAIO (AI Malaysia Berhad)**, the **Singapore AISI**, and the **UK AISI**, the model is subjected to standardized, containerized black-box and white-box evaluations inside the enclave:
   * **Agentic Scheming & Sandbagging Probes:** Testing whether the model behaves differently when it detects evaluation framing vs. deployment framing, attempts to disable oversight scripts, or strategically underperforms on capability benchmarks to evade regulatory thresholds.
   * **CBRN & Cyber Autonomy Benchmarks:** Evaluating uplift on biological design tasks and autonomous CTF/network penetration environments.
3. **The Enforcement Lever (Graduated Sanctions):** If a model fails red-line thresholds or if an operator tampers with Layer 1/2 telemetry, NAIO and MITI do not rely on slow court litigation. Under the **TNB Green Lane Conditional Power Supply Agreement**, TNB is contractually empowered to **throttle non-essential substation load allocation by 50–80%** (forcing the cluster down to maintenance/inference-only power ceilings), while MCMC suspends cross-border commercial API transit licenses and MITI revokes the operator’s Strategic Trade Permit.

## 5. Geopolitical & Economic Statecraft: The US–China–Malaysia Bargaining Model

Why would Washington, Beijing, and global hyperscalers accept Malaysia’s Safeguarded Compute Zone rather than boycotting Johor? Because Project SELAT solves an acute **Prisoner’s Dilemma** for all three actors:

### 1. Value Proposition to the United States (BIS / State Dept / DoD)
* **The U.S. Problem:** Washington lacks the inspection manpower to police global chip diversion and fears that capping allies and middle powers (as in the controversial AI Diffusion tiers) will push Southeast Asia and the Gulf into adopting Huawei Ascend stacks, permanently eroding U.S. CUDA ecosystem dominance.
* **The Malaysian Offer:** Project SELAT provides Washington with **sovereign-enforced, hardware-backed verification** at zero cost to U.S. taxpayers. Because Malaysia polices Johor clusters via TNB substation telemetry and Act 708 criminal penalties (up to RM30 million fine and imprisonment for WMD/strategic trade violations), the U.S. BIS can grant Johor Safeguarded Compute Zones **permanent Validated End-User (VEU) / Tier-1 uncapped status** for advanced U.S. GPUs.

### 2. Value Proposition to China & Chinese Commercial Tech Giants
* **The Chinese Problem:** Chinese commercial AI developers and cloud platforms (ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, rising open-weight labs) face severe domestic compute bottlenecks and risk arbitrary U.S. entity-list cutoffs when renting offshore cloud capacity.
* **The Malaysian Offer:** Malaysia refuses to enforce unilateral, nationality-based bans. Under Project SELAT, **any commercial tenant—American, Chinese, European, or ASEAN—is permitted to rent inference and verified training capacity in Johor on equal legal terms**, provided their workloads stay below proliferation thresholds and pass the same objective NAIO safety evaluations. Malaysia protects compliant commercial tenants from arbitrary seizure while guaranteeing Washington that no clandestine military or $>10^{26}$ FLOP proliferation runs occur.

### 3. Value Proposition to Malaysia & ASEAN (Sovereign Economic Statecraft)
* **Protecting the \$18B+ Johor Boom & RM25B NSS:** Avoids both U.S. chip caps (which would strand billions in TNB grid investments) and Chinese trade retaliation.
* **Upgrading from "Landlord" to "Governance Hub":** Instead of merely selling cheap land, water, and electricity to foreign tech giants, Malaysia captures high-margin **AI verification, safety auditing, hardware security packaging (in Penang), and sovereign cloud hosting** jobs—directly advancing Phase 2 and Phase 3 of the National Semiconductor Strategy.
* **The ASEAN–GCC Middle Power Coalition:** As 2025 ASEAN Chair and bridge to the Gulf Cooperation Council (ASEAN–GCC Summit), Malaysia can export the **SELAT Safeguards Standard** into a minilateral **Non-Aligned Compute Corridor** linking **Malaysia, Singapore, and the UAE**—creating a unified bloc controlling the dominant compute and transit hubs of the Global South.

## 7. Stress-Testing Key Risks & Countermeasures

1. **Risk: "Hyperscalers will simply bypass Johor and build in Thailand, Indonesia, or Vietnam."**
   * *Countermeasure:* AI training and low-latency regional cloud regions are bound by **dark-fiber latency to Singapore** (<2ms in Johor vs. 15–35ms in Bangkok or Jakarta), **grid reliability** (TNB’s 99.99% grid stability and 12-month Green Lane Pathway), and **existing sunk capital** (\$18B+ already under construction in Johor). Moreover, because Project SELAT *accelerates* US BIS chip export licensing and power hookups for compliant operators, operating inside a Malaysian Safeguarded Compute Zone lowers total time-to-deployment by 18–24 months compared to unverified neighboring jurisdictions.
2. **Risk: "Distributed training across multiple smaller data centers could evade single-substation power telemetry."**
   * *Countermeasure:* Multi-datacenter frontier training requires massive, sustained cross-facility optical bisection bandwidth (hundreds of terabits per second of synchronized gradient traffic). Because **Telekom Malaysia and MCMC** license all high-capacity terrestrial dark-fiber ducts between Johor campuses and across the Singapore causeway, cross-campus training traffic exhibits unmistakable synchronized bursts at the optical transport layer, which Layer 2 network attestation detects without inspecting payload contents.
3. **Risk: "Malaysia lacks the domestic technical talent to run frontier scheming and CBRN evaluations alone."**
   * *Countermeasure:* Project SELAT explicitly avoids reinventing the wheel. By structuring NAIO’s evaluation gate around **containerized Mutual-Recognition Evaluation Suites** co-developed with the **Singapore AISI, UK AISI, and independent evaluators (e.g., Equistamp / METR / Apollo Research)**, and funding local evaluator capacity through a **0.5% Compute Safeguards Levy** on Green Lane power tariffs, Malaysia piggybacks on global frontier evaluation science while retaining sovereign enforcement authority over the physical power switch.
